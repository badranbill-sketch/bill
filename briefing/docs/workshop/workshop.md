# How the workshop works

The visitor sees Bill on one side of the screen and a simple tool on the other. On a phone, Bill comes first, then the tool below him. There are four chapters. Each has one short recording and one activity. At the end, a summary, one short explanation picked from their answers, and an invitation they can ignore.

::: callout
**Three promises the scripts keep.** The numbers stay on the visitor's screen: Bill never sees them, and neither does anyone else. Skipping a question is always allowed and never counted as zero. Nothing at the end says whether someone "can retire".
:::

**The four activities**, each built on a drawing the site already uses:

| Chapter | Question | Activity | Drawing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What life do you want to fund? | An ordinary Tuesday | Notebook, glasses, cup |
| 2 | What income is already planned? | Planks on the bridge | Small bridge over water |
| 3 | What have you built? | The envelopes, and the house on its own shelf | Envelopes and notebook tabs |
| 4 | When do the pieces change? | Markers on the path | Winding path with markers |

The summary is **the map**: the path from chapter 4 with the planks, envelopes and Tuesday laid along it, the gaps drawn as gaps.

**Before recording.** The input labels and logic are frozen first; Bill records after. He records each language he chooses separately. English captions on a French recording do not count as an English recording.

::: callout
**What still needs a decision.** Two small activity features are not in the frozen workshop contract yet: the "one question" note in W00 and the Tuesday helper cards in chapter 1. Both stay on the visitor's device and never touch the numbers the model uses. They need a one-line contract addition and a review before they are built. Everything else fits the contract as it stands.
:::

# W00 · Welcome

*About 75 seconds. Plays first. Screen: the notebook drawing, and a single "Start" button that stays greyed for the first few seconds.*

**Spoken (EN)**

Hello. I'm Bill.

Before you start, three things.

This is four questions. What life do you want to fund. What income is already coming. What you've built. And when the pieces change. For each one, I'll tell you what I'd be looking for, and you fill in the tool beside me.

Your numbers stay on your screen. I don't see them. Nobody does. If you close the page, they're gone unless you choose to keep them on this device.

You don't need to know everything. When you're not sure, say so. "Not sure" is an answer here, and it's a useful one, because it shows us where the blanks are.

And this isn't a financial plan. At the end, you'll have a one-page map of where you stand and what's still missing. That's worth having on its own.

Before we start, write down one question you'd like to be clearer on by the end. Keep it. We'll come back to it.

**Parlé (FR)**

Bonjour. Je suis Bill.

Avant de commencer, trois choses.

Il y a quatre questions. Quelle vie voulez-vous financer. Quels revenus sont déjà prévus. Ce que vous avez accumulé. Et quand les choses changent. Pour chacune, je vous dis ce que je chercherais, et vous remplissez l'outil à côté de moi.

Vos chiffres restent sur votre écran. Je ne les vois pas. Personne ne les voit. Si vous fermez la page, ils disparaissent, à moins que vous choisissiez de les garder sur cet appareil.

Vous n'avez pas besoin de tout savoir. Quand vous n'êtes pas certain, dites-le. « Je ne sais pas », c'est une réponse ici, et elle est utile : elle montre où sont les blancs.

Et ce n'est pas un plan financier. À la fin, vous aurez une carte d'une page : où vous en êtes et ce qui manque encore. Ça vaut la peine en soi.

Avant de commencer, notez une question sur laquelle vous aimeriez y voir plus clair à la fin. Gardez-la. On y reviendra.

- **On screen:** 4 questions · Your numbers stay on your screen · "Not sure" is an answer
- **Activity: your question.** One short text box: "One question I'd like to be clearer on". It is shown again, untouched, at the top of the summary. Stored only on the device, never sent (needs the contract addition noted above).
- **Why this activity:** it gives the whole exercise a purpose the visitor chose, and it is the question they would bring to the 15-minute conversation if they want one.

# W01 · Chapter 1 — What life do you want to fund?

*About 70 seconds. Drawing: notebook, glasses and a cup.*

**Spoken (EN)**

Let's start with the life, not the money.

Picture an ordinary Tuesday, a year after you've stopped working. Not a holiday. A Tuesday. Where are you at ten in the morning? Who do you see? What does the day cost?

The cards beside me will help you build that week. Then turn it into one number: what you'd want to spend in a year, for your household.

Two details matter. Put it in today's dollars, what things cost now. The tool handles prices rising later. And think of it as money you spend after income tax, the way you'd think about a grocery bill.

Monthly or yearly, whichever is easier. If you have no idea, choose "not sure". We'll still be able to show you the rest.

**Parlé (FR)**

Commençons par la vie, pas par l'argent.

Imaginez un mardi ordinaire, un an après avoir arrêté de travailler. Pas des vacances. Un mardi. Où êtes-vous à dix heures du matin? Qui voyez-vous? Combien coûte la journée?

Les cartes à côté de moi vous aident à bâtir cette semaine. Ensuite, résumez-la en un chiffre : ce que votre ménage voudrait dépenser en une année.

Deux détails comptent. Pensez en dollars d'aujourd'hui, aux prix actuels. L'outil s'occupe de la hausse des prix plus tard. Et pensez à de l'argent dépensé après impôt, comme une facture d'épicerie.

Par mois ou par année, ce qui est plus simple. Si vous n'en avez aucune idée, choisissez « je ne sais pas ». On pourra quand même vous montrer le reste.

- **On screen:** An ordinary Tuesday · Household · Today's dollars · After tax
- **Activity: an ordinary Tuesday.** Cards the visitor can place on a week: *coffee out, the car, a hobby that costs something, the grandchildren, travel, helping family, the cottage, nothing in particular*. Each card placed opens one line: "roughly what does this cost in a month?" A running total appears in pencil at the bottom, which they can accept, change, or ignore. Then the real field: yearly or monthly household spending, or "not sure". One optional lifestyle word ("what are you retiring to?") stays local and does not touch the numbers.
- **Why this activity:** people estimate spending better from a pictured day than from a percentage, and the cards make the new costs of retirement visible (travel, hobbies) next to the ones that disappear.
- **Note:** the helper total is only a suggestion; the model uses the number the visitor enters.

# W02 · Chapter 2 — What income is already planned?

*About 80 seconds. Drawing: the small bridge over water.*

**Spoken (EN)**

Now the income that's already on its way. Think of it as planks on a bridge, from your last paycheque to the other side.

A pension from work. Your Quebec Pension Plan. Old Age Security. Maybe rent, or some part-time work.

For each plank, four things. Roughly how much. When it starts. Whether that amount is before tax or after tax, or you're not sure. And whether it's in today's dollars or the dollars of the year it starts.

If you don't know an amount, don't guess it. Leave the plank dashed. A dashed plank is honest. An invented one sends you across a bridge that isn't there.

One more thing. If an income is uncertain, like rent from a property, mark it that way. It's still useful. It's just not the same as a pension.

**Parlé (FR)**

Maintenant, les revenus déjà en route. Pensez-y comme aux planches d'un pont, entre votre dernière paie et l'autre rive.

Un régime de retraite d'employeur. La rente du Régime de rentes du Québec. La pension de la Sécurité de la vieillesse. Peut-être un loyer, ou un travail à temps partiel.

Pour chaque planche, quatre choses. À peu près combien. Quand ça commence. Si le montant est avant ou après impôt, ou si vous n'êtes pas certain. Et s'il est en dollars d'aujourd'hui ou en dollars de l'année où il commence.

Si vous ne connaissez pas un montant, ne le devinez pas. Laissez la planche en pointillé. Une planche en pointillé, c'est honnête. Une planche inventée vous fait traverser un pont qui n'existe pas.

Une dernière chose. Si un revenu est incertain, comme le loyer d'un immeuble, indiquez-le. Il reste utile. Mais ce n'est pas la même chose qu'une rente.

- **On screen:** Amount · Starts · Before or after tax? · Today's dollars?
- **Activity: planks on the bridge.** Each income added becomes a plank drawn across the bridge; its position shows when it starts. Unknown amounts or dates are drawn dashed. Uncertain income (rent, business, part-time) is drawn in a lighter line and never labelled "guaranteed". The visitor can tap "I've listed everything I know" or "there may be more".
- **Why this activity:** it makes a missing income visible as a gap instead of a silent zero, and it separates before-tax and after-tax amounts before anyone compares them to spending.

# W03 · Chapter 3 — What have you built?

*About 70 seconds. Drawing: organised envelopes and notebook tabs.*

**Spoken (EN)**

Now what you've set aside. Everything here is optional. You can skip the whole page and still get your map.

Think of envelopes. One for your RRSP or RRIF. One for your TFSA. One for money in a regular account. One for anything else. And one called "not sure", for the statement you haven't opened in a while.

Rough numbers are fine. No statements to upload. Nothing to connect.

The house goes on its own shelf, not in an envelope. It matters, but you can't pay the grocery bill with it unless you plan to sell or borrow against it, and that's a separate conversation.

And if you have a work pension that pays you monthly, it's already a plank on your bridge. Don't count it again here.

**Parlé (FR)**

Maintenant, ce que vous avez mis de côté. Tout ici est facultatif. Vous pouvez sauter la page au complet et avoir quand même votre carte.

Pensez à des enveloppes. Une pour votre REER ou votre FERR. Une pour votre CELI. Une pour l'argent dans un compte ordinaire. Une pour le reste. Et une « je ne sais pas », pour le relevé que vous n'avez pas ouvert depuis un moment.

Des chiffres approximatifs suffisent. Aucun relevé à téléverser. Rien à connecter.

La maison va sur sa propre tablette, pas dans une enveloppe. Elle compte, mais elle ne paie pas l'épicerie, à moins de prévoir la vendre ou emprunter sur sa valeur, et ça, c'est une autre conversation.

Et si vous avez un régime d'employeur qui vous verse une rente chaque mois, c'est déjà une planche de votre pont. Ne le comptez pas une deuxième fois ici.

- **On screen:** RRSP / RRIF · TFSA · Regular account · Other · Not sure · The house: its own shelf
- **Activity: the envelopes.** Five envelopes on a table; tapping one opens a single approximate amount (or "not sure"). The house sits on a shelf above them and is never added to the envelopes. If a pension appears both here and on the bridge, a small note asks which one to keep.
- **Why this activity:** it keeps tax categories apart (an RRSP dollar and a TFSA dollar are not the same spending money) and it stops the two most common double counts: the house and the pension.

# W04 · Chapter 4 — When do the pieces change?

*About 80 seconds. Drawing: the winding path with markers.*

**Spoken (EN)**

Last chapter. When do the pieces change?

Put markers on the path. Your age today. The age you'd like to stop working. When each income starts. If you share this with a partner, their dates too, because they're often not the same as yours.

Then two settings you'll see on screen, and I want to be clear about what they are.

The first is how many years of retirement the map shows. That's not a prediction of how long you'll live. It's just how far we draw the path.

The second is how fast prices rise. It's an assumption. Nobody knows the real number. Try a different one and watch what moves.

You can change any marker and see the map redraw. That's the point: to see which dates matter most for you.

**Parlé (FR)**

Dernier chapitre. Quand les choses changent-elles?

Placez des repères sur le chemin. Votre âge aujourd'hui. L'âge où vous aimeriez arrêter de travailler. Le début de chaque revenu. Si vous faites l'exercice à deux, les dates de votre conjoint aussi, parce qu'elles sont souvent différentes des vôtres.

Ensuite, deux réglages à l'écran, et je veux être clair sur ce qu'ils sont.

Le premier, c'est le nombre d'années de retraite que la carte montre. Ce n'est pas une prédiction de votre espérance de vie. C'est seulement jusqu'où on dessine le chemin.

Le deuxième, c'est la vitesse à laquelle les prix augmentent. C'est une hypothèse. Personne ne connaît le vrai chiffre. Essayez-en un autre et regardez ce qui bouge.

Vous pouvez déplacer n'importe quel repère et voir la carte se redessiner. C'est l'idée : voir quelles dates comptent le plus pour vous.

- **On screen:** Today · Stop working · Each income starts · Years shown (an assumption) · Price rise (an assumption)
- **Activity: markers on the path.** Draggable markers on the path drawing for current age, retirement age, each income start and, optionally, a partner's ages. Two plainly labelled assumption controls below the path: years shown, and price rise. Moving anything redraws the path immediately.
- **Why this activity:** dates change a retirement picture more than most balances do, and moving a marker shows that directly, without a lecture.

# W05 · Reading your map

*About 55 seconds. Plays when the summary appears.*

**Spoken (EN)**

This is your map.

Along the path: what you want to spend each year, and the income arriving to meet it. Where the planks don't cover the year, the gap is drawn as a gap. Where there's more than enough, that's shown separately.

Dashed lines are the things you weren't sure about. They matter more than anything else on this page, because they're the next things to find out.

At the bottom, the assumptions we used. Change one and the map changes. That's normal.

What this map can't tell you: taxes in detail, markets, your health, or whether you should retire on a given date. It shows the shape of the question.

Print it, save it, or keep it for a conversation. And look at the question you wrote at the start. Is it clearer now?

**Parlé (FR)**

Voici votre carte.

Le long du chemin : ce que vous voulez dépenser chaque année, et les revenus qui arrivent pour y répondre. Là où les planches ne couvrent pas l'année, l'écart est dessiné comme un écart. Là où il y a plus que suffisant, c'est montré à part.

Les pointillés, ce sont les choses dont vous n'étiez pas certain. Ce sont les plus importantes de la page, parce que ce sont les prochaines choses à vérifier.

En bas, les hypothèses utilisées. Changez-en une et la carte change. C'est normal.

Ce que cette carte ne peut pas vous dire : l'impôt en détail, les marchés, votre santé, ou si vous devriez prendre votre retraite à une date précise. Elle montre la forme de la question.

Imprimez-la, gardez-la, ou apportez-la à une conversation. Et relisez la question notée au départ. Est-elle plus claire?

- **On screen:** Gaps drawn as gaps · Dashed = still to find out · Assumptions below
- **Activity: the map.** The path with the Tuesday at the top, planks along it, envelopes beside it, gaps shaded, surplus years marked separately, unknowns dashed. A plain list underneath: "What to find out next", built from the dashed items. Print and save work entirely on the device.
- **Why this activity:** it turns the four chapters into one page someone can keep, and its most useful output is the list of blanks, not a number.

# The five endings

After the map, one short explanation plays. It is chosen automatically from the answers, by fixed rules, and the screen says so: *"This explanation was picked automatically from your answers. Bill hasn't seen them."* Missing information always takes priority over any projection. Only one plays; anything else noticed still shows on the map.

## W06 · Some income is still a dashed line

*About 40 seconds.*

**Spoken (EN)**

Some of your income is still a dashed line: an amount, or a start date, we don't have yet.

That's the most useful thing on your map, because it's the easiest to fix. Your Quebec Pension Plan estimate is available from Retraite Québec. A work pension has an annual statement. Old Age Security has its own rules for when it starts.

Fill in one dashed plank this week, come back, and watch the map redraw.

**Parlé (FR)**

Une partie de vos revenus est encore en pointillé : un montant, ou une date de début, qu'on n'a pas encore.

C'est la chose la plus utile de votre carte, parce que c'est la plus facile à régler. Retraite Québec peut vous donner une estimation de votre rente. Un régime d'employeur envoie un relevé annuel. La Sécurité de la vieillesse a ses propres règles de début.

Complétez une planche en pointillé cette semaine, revenez, et regardez la carte se redessiner.

- **On screen:** One dashed plank · Find one statement
- **Activity:** a short checklist of where each kind of estimate comes from, ticked on the device.
- **Note:** verify the exact Retraite Québec service name (online account and statement of participation) before recording.

## W07 · The map shows a gap in some years

*About 40 seconds.*

**Spoken (EN)**

In some years, the income you entered doesn't cover what you want to spend.

That's a picture of the figures you entered, nothing more. It isn't proof of a problem, and it doesn't mean you can't retire. It doesn't count savings, which may be meant for exactly those years.

Try this: move one marker. Your spending, or a start date. See which change closes the gap most. That tells you which question to look at first.

**Parlé (FR)**

Certaines années, les revenus entrés ne couvrent pas ce que vous voulez dépenser.

C'est l'image des chiffres entrés, rien de plus. Ce n'est pas la preuve d'un problème, et ça ne veut pas dire que vous ne pouvez pas prendre votre retraite. L'épargne n'y est pas comptée, alors qu'elle sert peut-être justement à ces années-là.

Essayez ceci : déplacez un repère. Vos dépenses, ou une date de début. Voyez quel changement réduit le plus l'écart. C'est la question à regarder en premier.

- **On screen:** A picture of your figures · Not a verdict · Move one marker
- **Activity:** "Move one thing": the markers and the spending field stay live; the gap years redraw.

## W08 · Before tax and after tax are mixed

*About 35 seconds.*

**Spoken (EN)**

Some of your income is before tax, or you weren't sure. Your spending is after tax.

Those can't be compared dollar for dollar. A $30,000 pension before tax is not $30,000 you can spend.

The next step is simple: for each plank, find out whether the amount is before or after tax. Your statements usually say.

**Parlé (FR)**

Une partie de vos revenus est avant impôt, ou vous n'étiez pas certain. Vos dépenses, elles, sont après impôt.

On ne peut pas les comparer dollar pour dollar. Une rente de 30 000 $ avant impôt, ce n'est pas 30 000 $ à dépenser.

La prochaine étape est simple : pour chaque planche, vérifiez si le montant est avant ou après impôt. Vos relevés l'indiquent en général.

- **On screen:** Before tax is not spendable · Check each plank
- **Activity:** each gross or unsure plank is highlighted with a "check this" flag the visitor can clear.
- **Note:** the $30,000 is an illustration, not a figure from the visitor.

## W09 · You and your partner stop at different times

*About 40 seconds.*

**Spoken (EN)**

You and your partner reach retirement in different years.

The years in between are their own stretch of the path: one of you has stopped, the other hasn't, and income and spending both change. The map can't see a partner's salary, so those years may look tighter than they are.

Draw those years on their own. Ask each other what they look like. It's a good conversation to have before either of you stops.

**Parlé (FR)**

Vous et votre conjoint arrivez à la retraite des années différentes.

Les années entre les deux sont un tronçon à part : l'un a arrêté, l'autre non, et les revenus comme les dépenses changent. La carte ne voit pas le salaire de votre conjoint, alors ces années peuvent paraître plus serrées qu'elles le sont.

Regardez ces années à part. Demandez-vous à quoi elles ressemblent. C'est une bonne conversation à avoir avant que l'un de vous arrête.

- **On screen:** The years in between
- **Activity:** the stretch between the two retirement markers is shaded on the path with the label "the years in between".

## W10 · Not enough to go on yet

*About 35 seconds. Also plays when nothing else applies.*

**Spoken (EN)**

There isn't enough here yet to draw a clear picture, and I'd rather say that than guess.

That's not a failure. It usually means one or two basics are missing: your spending, your retirement age, or an income.

Look at the list under your map. Pick the one blank you could fill most easily, and start there.

**Parlé (FR)**

Il n'y a pas encore assez d'information pour dessiner une image claire, et je préfère le dire plutôt que deviner.

Ce n'est pas un échec. Il manque souvent une ou deux choses de base : vos dépenses, l'âge de la retraite, ou un revenu.

Regardez la liste sous votre carte. Choisissez le blanc le plus facile à remplir, et commencez par là.

- **On screen:** Pick one blank
- **Activity:** the "What to find out next" list, with one item marked "easiest first" by the visitor.
- **Note:** when played as the fallback (map complete, nothing flagged), the first sentence is replaced by: "Nothing on your map needs a warning. What's left is the question you wrote at the start." The switch is decided by the same fixed rules.

# W11 · The invitation

*About 35 seconds. Optional; never required to see the map.*

**Spoken (EN)**

You've done the exercise. Whatever you do next, the map is yours.

If one question is still sitting there, you can bring it to me. Fifteen minutes, one question, no charge. You'd leave with a clearer question and the next thing to look at.

If it makes sense to work together after that, we'd talk about it. If not, that's fine.

**Parlé (FR)**

Vous avez fait l'exercice. Peu importe la suite, la carte vous appartient.

S'il vous reste une question, vous pouvez me l'apporter. Quinze minutes, une question, sans frais. Vous repartirez avec une question plus claire et la prochaine chose à regarder.

Si ça vaut la peine de travailler ensemble ensuite, on en parlera. Sinon, c'est correct.

- **On screen:** 15 minutes · One question · No charge
- **Activity:** the visitor's W00 question is shown beside the booking link. Booking sends nothing from the workshop: no numbers, no answers.
- **Note:** offer wording pending the firm reviewer (HB-24). "Online or in person" is added only when confirmed.
