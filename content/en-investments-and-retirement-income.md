To connect investments with retirement income, start with what the money needs to do. The conversation can then consider income sources, the timing of withdrawals, risks and costs. No single investment describes your whole situation.

## Begin with the need, before the product

Try asking your question without naming an investment: “What money might I need over the next few years?” or “Which expenses will other income cover?” It keeps what you need apart from how you might get it.

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada identifies financial circumstances, goals, investment time frame and risk tolerance as considerations before investing. [Read its investing basics](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/savings-investments/investing-basics.html).

## Map the questions over time

On a page, list expected income and dates that need checking. In another column, note the spending or plans for which you might use savings. Keep estimates visibly separate from facts you have confirmed.

Hypothetical example: someone would like to fund a project early in retirement while keeping part of their savings for later years. That raises questions about two different time frames. The example does not recommend an allocation, product or withdrawal amount.

## Look at what you actually hold

A list of account names can hide useful questions. What does each contain? What purpose is it expected to serve? Are there shared characteristics you had not noticed? What would you want explained before making a withdrawal? Bring those questions into the conversation rather than assuming that the number of accounts explains the risks.

## Include fees in the discussion

Depending on the investment, costs can arise when buying, selling or holding it. They can affect returns. FCAC’s investing-basics resource explains these categories.

Ask for an explanation of applicable fees and the services they cover. This website’s fee illustration explores two constant assumptions; it does not show Bill’s fees or tell you which option fits your needs. Its results exclude taxes, contributions and withdrawals, among other limitations.

## Agree on the next question to resolve

You do not need to settle every topic in the first conversation. Start with the information you most need: how an investment works, its costs, access to the money or how income sources fit together. Ask which available services address that question and on what terms.

This article does not set your withdrawal order. Tax effects and personal decisions need a closer look at your circumstances. No investment result or lasting income is guaranteed.
