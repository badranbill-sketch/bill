## Complete content, artwork and production inventory

This is the specification the agents must execute, not a claim that these scripts, drawings or recordings have already been made. Existing conversation drafts are inputs to revise; the final deliverables must stand alone in the repository and pass review.

### 1. The creative contract

Bill is a clear, confident person at a table, not a hype-driven influencer. He explains the next useful question, shows his reasoning and gives the viewer something they can use without booking him. The audience is adults near retirement, not a stereotype of helpless grandparents.

Navy ink, warm paper, editorial type, Bill's real portrait/recordings and a small physical retirement book tie the assets together. The landscape is ordinary life: home, chairs, notebook, path, bridge and dock. The brand line may appear on a final frame; do not force Bill to repeat a slogan in every video.

Obtain a 20-minute recorded voice interview with consent. Ask Bill how he would explain each question to someone across his desk, words he never uses, what he genuinely likes about this work, and how he handles uncertainty. This is source material, not a published testimonial. Remove any identifying client information before outside processing.

Each final asset carries: ID, locale, version, owner, status, source references for factual claims, rights, review hash and intended route/CTA. Status values: brief, draft, reviewed, recorded, edited, approved, published (where applicable). A file's existence never means it is published.

### 4. Ten Crossroads cases

A5 supplies case cards, A/B/C/D options, the selected answer or defensible answers, source notes, Bill's spoken explanation, one audience prompt and a score rule. All households are fictional. The round should teach one concept; do not stack several hidden tricks into a single question.

| ID | Fictional case seed | Question structure | Learning point |
|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Claire and Marc name a savings balance but no spending target | What information would make a first estimate more useful? Clear answer | Spending and income define the problem |
| C02 | A retiree removes commuting costs but adds travel and a renovation | Which budget separates recurring and one-time costs? Clear answer | Spending changes shape |
| C03 | Two people have equal savings but different pensions/start dates | Can equal balances support identical conclusions? Clear answer | Whole household context |
| C04 | Someone asks when to claim QPP without other facts | Which fact set is missing before a timing recommendation? Insufficient-information round | Avoid universal claiming advice |
| C05 | Mortgage payoff funded from different account types | Which comparison must include tax/liquidity? Clear planning principle, not a prescribed payoff | Choices have linked costs |
| C06 | A proposed retirement plan only shows average returns | Which additional scenario should be examined? Clear answer | Timing/sequence uncertainty |
| C07 | One partner retires years before the other | Which timeline is needed? Clear answer | Household bridge years |
| C08 | A household plans a large gift to an adult child | Which question connects the gift to their own needs? Clear answer | Generosity and sustainable priorities |
| C09 | Similar-looking RRSP/TFSA balances and a spending target | Can the whole total be treated as after-tax spending money? Clear answer | Tax bases are not interchangeable |
| C10 | Final case combines timing, spending, income and uncertainty | Choose the strongest next-step plan from four concrete proposals | Integrate the concepts without pretending to complete a financial plan |

Mix approximately six clear-principle rounds, two trade-off rounds and two insufficient-information rounds; the writer may adjust with reasons. Any answer involving legal/tax/benefit ages or amounts must be verified from current official sources. In particular, do not reuse generic CPP maximum-age examples as if they were identical QPP rules.

A surprise reveal becomes an unscored second question or was disclosed as a separate round. Never punish a participant for information deliberately withheld. No public leaderboard of financial competence. Ten points are game points only.

Required event assets: 10 round decks; host opening; transition lines; answer key; Q&A boundaries; local printable scorecard; closing book/meeting invitation; guest instructions; moderator checklist; recording disclosure; cancellation/outage message; approved replay description; invitation/reminder/follow-up copy. Include a 60-minute rehearsable run-of-show.

### 9. The 90-day calendar

Use a rolling 90-day cycle from the actual launch date, not an invented 18-week quarter. Two posts per seven-day block, with the final block shortened: proposed day numbers 2 and 5, then add seven each week through days 86 and 89. One article on day 3 + seven each week through day 87. These are schedule proposals and can be adapted around actual holidays/events without doubling sends.

Each calendar row includes asset/version, locale, primary channel, date/time, destination, CTA, review gate and actual publication ID. Crossroads dates are entered by Bill; reminders derive from each event's real date. Do not publish ads for an unscheduled event or link to an unapproved article.
